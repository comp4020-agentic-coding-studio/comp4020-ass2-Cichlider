The model is not a substitute for judgement. It is a shared vocabulary for a
domain most people only ever discuss in the first person — how *I* feel,
what *I* want — which is precise about experience and useless for comparing
one situation to another. This course treats a relationship the way a
systems course treats any two-party arrangement: two agents, each holding
information the other doesn't, transacting under uncertainty.

That reframe is the whole first move. "Do they like me" is a first-person
question with no stable answer. "What is the current state of information
asymmetry between these two parties" is a systems question, and it has a
method.

## Outline

- what counts as a "position" versus a feeling, and why the distinction matters
- the two-party frame: each side holds private information the other is
  pricing in
- the semester's arc — attraction, dependency, commitment, monitoring, risk —
  and how each week adds one variable to the model, never two
- what a case review is: bring a real or composite situation, leave with a
  clearer statement of it, not a verdict
