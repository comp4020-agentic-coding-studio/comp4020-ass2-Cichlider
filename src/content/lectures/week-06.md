Trust doesn't get established by a statement ("you can trust me") — it gets
established by a sequence of small, reversible tests, each cheap enough to
survive a failed result: a minor favour asked and granted, a small
vulnerability disclosed and handled well, a plan proposed and honoured. Each
passed test raises the stakes a party is willing to risk on the next one.
That ladder is reciprocal investment, and it's a separate variable from
reliability — reliability is "did they show up," reciprocity is "did they
show up for something that cost them something."

The failure mode this week names is asymmetric test proposal: one party
keeps initiating tests (asking favours, disclosing first, proposing plans)
and the other keeps accepting the benefit without ever proposing one back.
That pattern is measurable, and it's a better predictor of an unequal
relationship than either party's stated feelings about fairness.

## Outline

- trust as a ladder of small reversible tests, not a declared quantity
- distinguishing a passed test from a low-stakes non-event
- measuring reciprocity: who proposes the tests, and who only responds to
  them
- reading for next week: what happens once a party stops testing and asks
  for exclusivity instead
