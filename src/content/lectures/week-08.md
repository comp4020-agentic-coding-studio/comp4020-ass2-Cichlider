A situationship is what a stalled commitment gate becomes once it stops
being a delay and starts being a stable equilibrium — both parties keep
transacting at the pre-commitment price indefinitely, because neither has an
incentive to be the one who forces a repricing. The model names three
distinct routes into that equilibrium: the *asymmetric-cost* case (repricing
would cost one party much more than the other, so that party avoids raising
it), the *sunk-specificity* case (leaving would forfeit specific value built
up in weeks 4–5, which anchors both parties to the status quo even though
neither is satisfied by it), and the *silent-substitute* case (one party is
quietly keeping the option open elsewhere, which removes their incentive to
close the gate on this side).

Telling these apart matters because the fix is different for each: the
asymmetric-cost case needs the higher-cost party to name the cost out loud;
the sunk-specificity case needs both parties to separate "what I've already
built here" from "what continuing is actually worth"; the silent-substitute
case needs the other party to notice they are competing against an option
they don't know exists.
