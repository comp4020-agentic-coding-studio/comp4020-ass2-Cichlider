Exclusivity is an option, not a formality. Taking it means giving up the
right to pursue other positions, in exchange for a claim on this one — and
like any option, it's rational to price before exercising, not after.
The commitment gate is the point in a case where that pricing has to happen
explicitly, usually because one party has proposed it and the other now has
to answer.

Most stalled cases stall here, and the model's diagnosis is usually one of
three things: the option is priced correctly by both sides and one party is
simply still deciding (not a failure, a delay); the two parties are pricing
it differently and haven't said so out loud; or one party has already been
behaving as if the option were exercised, without the other having agreed to
sell it. That third pattern is the one worth naming early, because it's the
one most likely to end badly for whoever assumed it.
