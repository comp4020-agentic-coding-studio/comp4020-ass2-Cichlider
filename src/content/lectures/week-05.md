Early on, most of what a relationship provides is fungible — someone to talk
to, someone to do things with, categories a reasonable number of people
could fill. Object specificity is the process by which those categories
narrow to one name: the shared history, in-jokes, and calibrated
understanding of a particular person become a large share of what's actually
being provided, and no substitute delivers it at the same price.

This is the variable that makes "there are plenty of fish in the sea" true
early and false later. It isn't false because the sea empties; it's false
because the thing that mattered stopped being generically available and
started being specific to one counterparty. Recognising when a case has
crossed that line is most of this week's work, and most of the position
paper due this week.
