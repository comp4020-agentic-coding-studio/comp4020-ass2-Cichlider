A stop-loss is a condition, named in advance, under which a party exits a
position regardless of how attached they've become by the time the
condition triggers. Naming it in advance matters because dependency (week 4)
actively degrades a party's ability to name it in the moment — the balance
sheet that makes leaving costly is the same one that makes leaving hard to
evaluate clearly once the moment arrives. A stop-loss set before entanglement
is a promise to a version of yourself who could still see clearly, made to a
version who might not be able to later.

A black swan is different: not a threshold crossed, but an event outside the
model's assumptions entirely — the kind of thing no stop-loss was written to
cover because nobody thought to write it. The Black Swan Protocol is not a
prediction method; it's a standing procedure (who you talk to, what you
check, what you don't decide alone) for the position to fall back on when
the model runs out of coverage.
