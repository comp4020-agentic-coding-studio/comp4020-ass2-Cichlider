A single good date is a spot transaction. A relationship is a recurring
contract, and recurring contracts are priced on a variable spot transactions
don't have: reliability. Does this party show up when they say they will,
respond inside a range you can predict, hold a plan once it's made? None of
that is romantic. All of it is the thing that turns intermittent attraction
into a system you can actually plan around.

The mistake this week targets is treating reliability as a personality trait
("they're just flaky") instead of a measurable rate. A rate can go up. A
personality trait, in most people's folk model, can't — which is why "they're
flaky" ends a conversation that "they've missed three of the last five
plans" should actually start.

## Outline

- spot transaction versus recurring contract: what changes once you expect

- reliability as an observed rate, not an inferred trait
- why reliability compounds: each kept commitment lowers the cost of
