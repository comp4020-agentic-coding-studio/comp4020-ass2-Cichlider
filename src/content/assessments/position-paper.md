## The brief

> Classify one relationship against the model's first five variables, and
> show your working for each.

Weeks 1 through 5 gave you five variables: the two-party framing, the
attraction front-end/back-end split, the reliability rate, the five-line-item
dependency balance sheet, and the fungible/specific provision split. This
paper asks you to apply all five to one case, in order, and to defend each
classification with something a reader could check independently — a
computed rate, a listed line item score, a named substitute that either
does or doesn't exist.

The provocation is not "write about a relationship." It's "show that a
stranger, reading only your evidence, would reach the same classification
you did." A position paper that reports conclusions without the working
behind them will not clear the model-application criterion, however correct
the conclusions turn out to be.

## What you submit

A single PDF, 1200–1800 words, structured variable by variable (five
sections, one per variable, each stating the classification and the
evidence for it). De-identify or composite any third party's private
details — the model doesn't need a true story, it needs a checkable one.

Model application (60%) is marked on whether each of the five variables is
applied correctly and specifically to the case. Clarity of reasoning (40%)
is marked on whether a reader unfamiliar with the case could follow the
argument from evidence to classification without needing the writer present
to explain it.
