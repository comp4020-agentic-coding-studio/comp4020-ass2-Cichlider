# Policies and support

## Late submission

Work submitted late is charged a holding cost: 5% of the assessment's total
weight per day late, for up to five days, after which the position is
closed at zero. This is a mechanical rate, not a judgement of the work —
the same rate applies whether the work is finished or half-done, because
the policy prices the delay, not the content.

## Extensions

An extension is a repriced deadline, agreed in advance of the original one
wherever possible. Request one by emailing the convenor with the specific
new date requested and the reason; "as much time as I can get" is not a
request the policy can process, because it doesn't name a condition under
which the extension ends. Extensions requested after the original deadline
has passed are treated as a late submission until agreed otherwise —
requesting one is not, by itself, a stop on the late-submission clock.

## Academic integrity

Every piece of submitted work must be your own analysis, applying the
model to a case you have the standing to write about. Composited or
de-identified cases are expected and encouraged, per the assessment briefs
— what isn't permitted is presenting someone else's classification of a
case as your own, or fabricating a case's evidence to fit a cleaner
narrative than the real one supports. Use of AI assistance must be declared
in a short note attached to the submission, naming what it was used for;
undeclared assistance is treated as an integrity breach, not as a marking
deduction.

## Getting help

Case reviews are where a stuck classification gets unstuck — bring the
specific point you're unsure about, not a request to have the whole case
worked for you. Office hours (see the [people page](/people/)) are for
disputing a mark or working through something that doesn't fit a case
review's time. For anything that can't wait for either, email the relevant
teacher directly; a same-day response is a courtesy, not a guarantee.
