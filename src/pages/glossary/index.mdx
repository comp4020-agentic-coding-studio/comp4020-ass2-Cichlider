# Glossary and risk disclosures

This page is reference material, not required weekly reading. It exists for
students who want the model's variables named precisely, and it carries the
disclosures the model comes with — read once, at the point they become
relevant to a real case, not skimmed in week 1.

## Variable notation

The lectures avoid notation because the prose doesn't need it. Use the table
below only when a discussion needs to distinguish two states more precisely
than a sentence conveniently does.

| Symbol | Name | The question it answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| V | Perceived value | Is this person worth continued contact? |
| A | Attention allocation | Do I keep thinking about and seeking them out? |
| R | Romantic/physical approach | Do I want romantic or physical contact? |
| S | Object specificity | Do I want a person like this, or specifically them? |
| D | Dependency | Which of my outcomes now require their participation? |
| J | Joint investment | How much have we built that can't be moved without loss? |
| E | Exclusivity agreement | Have we agreed to stop developing with others? |
| I | Identity | Do we actually treat each other as partners, in planning and self-narrative, not just in label? |

The full pipeline the course covers, in this notation, is a strictly
increasing dependency chain, not eight independent scores:

```
V → A/R (attraction, weeks 2-3)
  → repeated, reliable interaction (week 3)
  → D (dependency, week 4)
  → S (specificity, week 5)
  → reciprocal small-scale investment (week 6)
  → E (the commitment gate, week 7)
  → J (expanded joint investment, weeks 9-10)
  → I (partner identity, week 12)
```

Each arrow is a probability increase, not a guarantee. A case can stop at
any arrow — see Disclosure 1, below.

## Two strategies, not one relationship type

The model prices two different strategies, and most misreadings of a case
come from applying one strategy's success criteria to the other.

**Short-term.** The objective is to exchange romance, contact and novelty
under low commitment and low joint investment. It still requires real
inputs — mutual attraction, a stated romantic intent, clear boundaries, an
explicit position on exclusivity and communication frequency, and a
willingness to close the position promptly once goals diverge. Its main
failure mode is not the absence of dependency; it's letting emotional
dependency (D) run ahead of the low-commitment rules the position was
opened under.

**Long-term.** The objective is to convert two independently viable people
into a reliable, reciprocal, jointly profitable two-party system. It
requires reliable presence, shared routines, bidirectional disclosure,
conflict repair, continually expanding joint investment (J), and renegotiating
the relationship's rules as soon as real dependency has outgrown them. A
short-term position is not an unfinished long-term one — they are different
instruments with different entry requirements, and treating the first as a
lesser version of the second misprices both.

## Risk disclosures

1. **The pipeline is not mandatory or guaranteed.** Every arrow above raises
   the probability of the next state; none of them force it. A case
   "stalling" between two states is a normal outcome, not evidence that
   something is broken — see week 8.
2. **Sequencing varies by context.** The order in which exclusivity, partner
   labels and cohabitation happen is not fixed across cultures or
   communities. Treat the week 2-12 ordering as this course's reference case,
   not a universal spec.
3. **High dependency is not, by itself, a health signal.** Dependency
   produced by inconsistency, isolation from other relationships, or a
   manufactured inability to exit reads as high D on a naive score and
   produces real difficulty leaving — but it is not the reciprocal, reliable
   dependency this model treats as valuable. Score the source of a
   dependency, not only its magnitude.
4. **Non-exclusive structures can still protect joint investment.** Exclusivity
   (E) is one way to protect J; explicit, consented boundaries in a
   non-monogamous structure can do the same job. The model prices the
   protection, not the specific rule that provides it.
5. **Behavioural signals update an estimate; they do not read a mind.** Every
   signal in week 10's audit narrows uncertainty about someone's position.
   None of them, singly or combined, proves what someone feels. A low signal
   can also mean low capacity or a constrained situation, not low interest —
   distinguish these before pricing an exit.
